How to play

  • Free tile rule: a tile is free only when nothing sits on top of it (no higher-layer tile overlaps it at all) and its left or right long side is open — no same-layer neighbour on at least one side. Tiles above or below it in the same layer do not block. Free tiles are bright, outlined in teal, and carry a small dot.
  • Matching: two free tiles match when their faces are identical. The one exception: any Flower matches any Flower, and any Season matches any Season — but a Flower never matches a Season.
  • Choose pairs carefully: ordinary faces have four copies, so which two you pair matters — the wrong pair can bury a tile you need later.
  • Win: clear every tile. Blocked: tiles remain but no free matching pair exists — shuffle, undo, or start a new deal.
  • Triple (rule variant): select three free tiles of the same face (or three Flowers, or three Seasons). The first two stay selected; a non-matching third tap replaces the oldest selection.
  • Countdown (rule variant): the Turtle band against a 10:00 clock. Reaching 0:00 with tiles left ends the run — the board stays viewable and no score is kept.
  • Avalanche (rule variant): after each pair is removed, the tile directly above a vacated slot in the same column slides down into it when that slot is free — and the tile above that one slides down in turn — which can free new tiles.

How each deal is made

Deals are never made by shuffling faces at random — about 3% of random Turtle deals cannot be solved at all. Instead, every board here is reverse-dealt:

  • First the seed draws the layout band (Turtle, Quick, …), then the rule variant, then the deal — so every time Mahjong is picked, the player meets a fresh combination of band, rules and deal. A daily puzzle pins one of each, so everyone plays the same board that day.
  • Start from the empty layout and repeatedly remove two (three in Triple) currently-free positions, logging the order. That log is the removal witness.
  • Walk the log backwards, laying one matching pair (or triple) of faces at each step.
  • Before you play, the page replays the witness against the dealt faces. Only a deal whose replay clears the board is shown; a failed seed is rejected and the next seed is dealt instead (rejections are counted in the notes below).

Faces come from a small seeded RNG (mulberry32) so a seed always deals the same board — the same seed also always draws the same layout band. Shuffle re-runs the same reverse-builder over the surviving tiles, so a shuffled board is still guaranteed solvable. Scoring (demo only): 100 per pair, 1,000 clear bonus, plus 5 per second under par — par 180s on the Quick 56 band, 600s on the 144-tile bands.

Layout is a band — locked

  • The decision: a layout (Turtle, Fortress, Quick, …) is a band — the same word Nonogram uses for its sizes. Bands are drawn from the seed, hidden from the player as a catalogue, never a leaderboard board, and never counted toward the 100+ variants. Only rule changes count as variants.
  • Why: a Fortress board under the same rules is the same puzzle as a Turtle board in a different shape — same matching, same free-tile rule, same win. The reference site behind the owner's screenshot, themahjong.com, lists 273 layouts (its picker: All 273 = New 112 + Featured 17 + Other 144), every one played under a single ruleset — hundreds of layouts, one game. Counting layouts as variants would be exactly that inflation.
  • What the generator does with bands: seed → layout band → rule variant → reverse-dealt deal. In this demo, Classic draws its band from the seed on every New Deal (Turtle 144 or Quick 56); the other boards pin their band so the rule change is easy to compare. In Morphi, the daily seed pins band + variant + deal for the day.
  • Adding bands later: a layout joins the band pool only when its coordinates are frozen and its reverse-deal check passes — Turtle, Quick and Fortress (144) are the starting pool; the other themahjong.com layouts (Dragon, Spider, Pyramid, Crab, Cat — all 144 there) are candidates, not copies: each needs its own frozen coordinates before it can ship.
  • The Fortress board (sixth board, new): pins the Fortress band under classic rules so the shape can be judged on its own. The shape is Morphi-defined — the research found no canonical Fortress (published counts conflict: 144, 114, 132 and 80 tiles) — drawn here as a courtyard floor ringed by a wall, with corner towers three layers deep and a raised centre keep: 92 + 40 + 12 = 144 tiles across 3 layers. Decision A8 stays open until the owner approves this shape; only then are its coordinates frozen for the real build.
  • Tile faces: traditional-style faces drawn originally for this demo as inline SVG — Characters (numeral over 萬), Bamboo sticks, Circles, the four Winds, the three Dragons, Flowers and Seasons — on ivory tiles with a deep embossed bevel, a thick green side edge and a cast shadow, like a real tile. Every tile is fully opaque on every layer, as on themahjong.com — depth comes from the layer offset and shadow only, never from transparency; a covered tile is muted with a solid darker ivory body, not by being see-through. No other site's tile art is copied. English face names survive only as screen-reader labels, never printed on the tile.

Where the demo matches the researched rules

  • Free-tile rule exactly as researched: no tile on top (any overlap from a higher layer) and left or right long side open; same-layer tiles above/below never block.
  • Matching: identical faces only, except any Flower matches any Flower and any Season matches any Season; a Flower never matches a Season.
  • Standard 144-tile set on the Turtle boards: 42 distinct faces — 108 suited (Circles, Bamboo, Characters 1–9 × 4), 28 honours (4 Winds, 3 Dragons × 4), 8 bonus (4 Flowers, 4 Seasons, one each). Turtle layer split 87 + 36 + 16 + 4 + 1, per the research report.
  • Generation by reverse-dealing with a removal witness, replayed in-page before the deal is shown — the fix the research recommends, since ~2.95–3% of randomly shuffled Turtle deals are provably unsolvable (de Bondt's analysis of ten million deals).
  • Win = all tiles cleared; blocked = tiles remain with no free matching pair, board stays viewable, with Shuffle / Undo / Restart / New Deal offered. Quick 56 is a size band (a Morphi teaching board), not a rule variant — no canonical 56-tile board exists in the research.
  • Layout is a band (locked 2026-11-10): the generator draws the band from the seed before dealing; bands are never variants, never counted, never a board. Turtle / Quick / Fortress are bands. The 100+ count comes from rule changes only.
  • Fortress band (Morphi-defined, A8 open): 144 tiles in 3 layers (92 + 40 + 12) — a courtyard floor ringed by a wall, three-tile-deep corner towers and a centre keep, in the 3-layer form Mahjong Titans uses for Fortress. Published Fortress counts conflict in the research (Titans 144, Mahjong Bay 114, PlayInPhone 132, mahjong-solitaire.me 80), so no published board is copied. It reverse-deals with a removal witness and is witness-validated before it is shown, exactly like the other boards, under the same classic pair rules and the standard 144-tile set.
  • Faces are traditional-style, drawn originally as inline SVG (Characters, Bamboo, Circles, Winds, Dragons, Flowers, Seasons) — the earlier text-label tiles ("3 CIRCLES", "EAST WIND") were rejected by the owner as not reading as Mahjong; the demo is accepted as a working prototype on its rules and generation.
  • Triple (match three) and Avalanche (tiles above a cleared pair drop down) are genuine rule variants found in the research; Countdown is the researched timed mode (Jambolaya offers 5- and 10-minute Turtle timers).
  • Animation budget (A5, revised): the entrance is one flat 2 seconds on every board. A new deal first shows the board empty, then the tiles appear one by one, layer by layer — all of layer 0 first, then layer 1, then 2, 3 and so on — each tile popping in at its own slot, and taps are held until the entrance completes, so the finished board never flashes on screen first and nothing can be tapped before it appears. Each layer gets an equal share of the 2 seconds: the layers hold very different numbers of tiles (Turtle 87 / 36 / 16 / 4 / 1), so the old single per-tile interval made each higher layer fill faster than the one below it, on every board — now every layer takes the same time to fill. A Shuffle's tile movement still finishes within 1 second, a matched pair or triple eases out and fades over 500ms before it leaves the board, and Avalanche drops slide down into place. With reduced motion — the system setting or the toggle above the board — all of it is switched off and tiles appear, move and leave instantly.
  • Tiles are solid and embossed: no tile uses transparency on any layer (the earlier 50% fade on blocked tiles made upper layers look ghostly and confusing). Blocked tiles keep a solid, slightly darker ivory body with a muted face; free tiles are brighter. Every tile carries a raised bevel — bright top highlight, inner shading, a 6px green bottom wall (thicker on higher layers) and a layered cast shadow — so stacked tiles read as physical tiles, as on the reference site.

Where the demo differs

  • Demo RNG is mulberry32; production uses SplitMix64.
  • Demo Shuffle is unlimited in Classic (implementations in the wild usually limit it to 1–3 per game, sometimes at a point cost); demo Shuffle also clears the undo stack.
  • Demo scoring (100 per pair, 1,000 clear bonus, 5 per second under par) is illustrative only — the research found no standard scoring system for Mahjong Solitaire.
  • The demo has no ranked timing, no server replay, and no daily board.
  • Avalanche drop rule is a Morphi definition: the tile directly above a just-vacated slot in the same layer and column drops one tile-step into it when the slot is still empty, cascading up the column within the same move. (A removed tile is free by definition, so nothing can rest on top of it; the researched Avalanche mode — "tiles above slide down when a pair below is cleared" — is therefore realised as this column slide.) Drops are recorded, so Undo reverses them exactly.
  • Countdown and blocked runs keep no score in the demo, matching the researched "timeout ends the run" behaviour.

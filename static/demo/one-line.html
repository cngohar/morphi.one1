How to play

  1. 1

    Start on a ringed dot. If two dots wear orange rings, the stroke has to start on one of them. No rings — start anywhere.

  2. 2

    Draw every line exactly once in one continuous stroke, dot to dot. Drag back along a line, or tap Undo, to take a line back.

  3. 3

    End on the other ringed dot — or back where you started, when no dots are ringed.

What this demo is testing

Centered, not full-screen. The 12 Nov 2026 global research found no sourced One-line game that needs pan or zoom — every sourced game fits the whole figure to the phone screen, so the report's verdict is that this puzzle fits Morphi's centered stage, not the full stage. This demo tests exactly that: the whole board stays visible in a centered column, largest figure 17 lines, and even on a 360px-wide phone the grid dots sit about 70px apart — above the 44–48pt tap-target guidance the research cites. Full stage would only become a sourced need at Expert scale (35–60 lines, Lakolam), which no sourced game pairs with pan/zoom.

The rule underneath

A connected figure can be drawn in one stroke exactly when it has 0 or 2 odd dots (dots with an odd number of lines). 0 odd = start anywhere, finish where you began; 2 odd = you must start on one ringed dot and end on the other; more than 2 = impossible. Euler proved the Königsberg case in 1736: 7 bridges, 4 land masses with degrees 3, 3, 3, 5 — four odd, so no route exists. Try it in the last figure.

Scoring would be a Morphi invention

Classic play is untimed — no timer, no lives, no game over. The market scores by stars and hints: One Line Galaxy gives three stars for finishing without hints. The closest sourced daily, Constellation Weaver, scores 100 points per thread, minus a quarter per stroke above goal, plus a time bonus capped at 500, resetting at midnight UTC. This demo's stars (3 = no hints, 2 = one hint, 1 = more) follow the market pattern, not an established One-line formula — no published par times were found.

Source: One Line Galaxy Source: Constellation Weaver

Seeded daily fits; variants unproven

Solvability is checkable before a daily ships: parity (0/2 odd) plus connectivity, with a Hierholzer trail as a linear-time witness. Lakolam's generator is deterministic per seed, stores its trail, and re-walks it to verify. VEXO already runs a daily that is “the same for every player worldwide”, one attempt, with a timer and a daily leaderboard; its fastest reported daily solves were 5.8s, 9.5s and 13.4s. But genuine variants are the gap: the research lists 24 candidates (one-way lines, double lines, must-return-home, two-stroke, spot-the-impossible, repair-one-edge…) and no source enumerates 100+ — that count is not proven.

Open questions the research leaves

  • Lift rule: sources split — Trace Once resets the board when the finger lifts; Block Link and MangoFun's One Line let you lift and resume. This demo uses lift-and-resume (drag back along a line, or Undo, to take a line back) — a choice, not a settled standard.
  • Dead ends: classic play has no lose state — getting stuck means a route stranded part of the figure, and the remedy is undo or restart. Whether Morphi should call that a formal “lose” is undecided. This demo only flags it and lets you recover.
  • 100+ variants: not proven by any source. Reaching 100+ would rest on combining the 24 candidate rule changes, which no source enumerates — the variant matrix still has to be built and tested.
  • Daily format: no source settles the daily size band (Lakolam: Kids 6–10, Easy 11–16, Medium 17–24, Hard 25–34, Expert 35–60 lines), whether hints/undo are allowed in a ranked daily, or how ties break when many valid trails solve in similar seconds — the trail is “one of many”; only the solvable/impossible verdict is unique.
  • Name: generic wording (“one-line”, “one-stroke”) looks safe and the mechanic is public domain (Euler, 1736), but no formal USPTO/EUIPO trademark clearance was done — that is a research finding, not legal clearance.

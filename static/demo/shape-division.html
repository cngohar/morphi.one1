Shape Division

One empty shape. Cut it into identical pieces — same size and same shape. Tap two points and the cut runs along the grid between them. Any division that makes identical pieces wins. (Equal area is the exception: there, pieces only need the same size.)

Dev presets (picker is demo-only — the player surface never shows variant names)

Tap a point to start a cut, then tap a second point — the cut appears along the grid between them, exactly where you tapped. The faint dots are the grid points you can tap. A straight cut is one pair of taps; if a cut turns, tap the corner where it turns, then tap on. To remove a cut, tap its two end points again. A swipe from one point to another works as the same two taps. On the square grid there is no diagonal grid line — the dots on a diagonal are joined only by horizontal and vertical lines — so tapping opposite corners makes a stepped cut along the grid lines instead, and the dashed preview shows exactly where it will run before you tap. A corner point itself sits on the outline: the route follows the outline to the nearest grid line, and only the grid-line part becomes the cut. You can keep cutting pieces that have already come free. Any piece you cut free lifts in place at once, in its own colour, identical or not — Check is what judges it: pieces must be the same size and the same shape (except on Equal area, where only the size must match). One square is the smallest piece there is.

About this demo (owner review)

How v10 works: the board is one empty outline — no cells are shown, but a faint dot marks every grid point. You cut by tapping two points: tap where a cut starts, tap where it ends, and the cut runs along the grid edges between them (the straightest route — a cut that turns is built by tapping the corner where it turns). Tapping the same two points again removes the cut, and a swipe counts as its two end points tapped in order. Cutting is free-form: any region your cuts separate from the rest of the shape detaches at once — whole piece, outline and fill together — whether it is identical or not. The checker is property-based, not answer-matching, and judges only at Check: any division into the required number of identical (same size and same shape), connected pieces that covers the shape wins, and a non-identical division fails Check and counts a mistake. Equal area is the exception: there only the sizes must match. Undo puts the last detached piece back.

What v5 fixes: detached pieces no longer slide outward — they lift in place with a small inset gap, so the board stays one aligned shape instead of an exploded pile of overlapping boxes, and a cut that separates two pieces is drawn once (as the pieces' own outlines), not twice. Dragging now also samples the grid line nearest the finger, so a fast stroke — or a stroke across a piece that already came free — actually cuts; and a tap anywhere on a piece toggles its nearest cut (a single square's centre used to fall in a dead zone). The two-column layout now fills the whole screen: the board stretches to the available height and the side panel scrolls internally if it needs to.

What v6 fixes: cutting a piece that had already come free stopped working once the board stretched to fill its column. The stretched svg letterboxes the drawing (centred, with empty bands), but the pointer was still mapped linearly across the whole element, so a drag across a visible piece landed whole cells away from the finger and no cut registered. The pointer is now mapped through the letterbox, so drags and taps land where the finger actually is — on the main shape and on detached pieces alike, and any piece can be cut into smaller pieces.

What v7 fixes: dragging a straight cut, a small sideways slip of the mouse used to drop in a short vertical cut that was never planned (the owner's screenshot showed exactly that spur on a horizontal stroke). The old code snapped on every pointer movement and also added any nearby grid line that touched the stroke, so passing a junction with a slight slip added the vertical line. The stroke now locks to one direction per segment — a segment is only drawn when the pointer moves clearly along that line and travels most of the way across it — slips are ignored, the dashed line is only a preview, and the cut is committed on release. Turning at a corner and backtracking along your own line still work. No drawing library was needed: the lock is small geometry against the grid edges the board already has, and a freehand library would still need that same grid lock on top.

What v8 fixes: on Shape given the Classic drag fix was not enough — whole pieces could not be cut at all and a line drawn above the 58% label never appeared. Three causes, all in the v7 vertex-walk: (1) the walk could only travel along connected cut edges, so a stroke across the top of the ∩-shaped piece stalled at the notch — the segment over the notch borders an empty cell and is not a cut edge, so the far side was never reached; (2) a stroke had to start within 0.62 cells of a grid vertex, but the centre of a cell — where a piece's % label sits, and where a player naturally starts — is 0.707 cells from every vertex, so strokes starting on the 25% bar or an 8% single cell never anchored; (3) the walk rejected travel more than ~0.38 cells from a grid line, and drawing across a one-cell-wide bar runs down its centreline, a full 0.5 cells away. The walk is replaced by stroke matching: the finished stroke is compared with the board's edges directly — an edge is cut if the stroke runs along it (most of its span, within half a cell, travelling along it rather than forth-and-back, which is what still rejects a sideways slip) or slashes straight across it (through its middle and past the far side, which is how a one-cell-wide bar is chopped by a stroke down its middle). A stroke running between two parallel lines cuts only the nearer one. Matching runs live for the dashed preview — the player's own stroke also shows faintly, so a stroke is never invisible — and the cut is committed on release, as before.

What v9 fixes: on Equality score, pressing Check before the division was complete counted as a mistake (−50 each, and three such Checks ended the run) and gave no equality reading at all — the owner's screenshot showed that dead end at 50% progress, asking why they could not check themself. The variant is no pass/fail by its own rule, so Check is now a self-check at any point: it reports the equality % of the pieces formed so far, lists each piece's share, and says how many more pieces to cut (or that there are too many) — it costs nothing and the run continues. Only a complete 4-piece division ends the run, scored by its equality % as before.

What v10 changes: the freehand drag is gone, replaced by tap-two-points cutting (owner's pick after the drag kept failing in playtests). Every drag version had to guess which grid line a wobbly finger path meant — v7's vertex-walk stalled at notches and had a centre dead zone, v8's stroke matching fixed those and still depended on interpreting a path. Tapping never guesses: the player names the exact two grid points, the cut follows the grid edges between them (Dijkstra over the edge graph with a turn penalty, so straight stays straight), re-tapping a pair removes the cut, and each pair is a single Undo step. On the square grid, corner-to-corner is not a grid line, so that tap pair resolves to one clean turn. Faint dots now mark every tappable grid point, and a status line under the board confirms the first point and explains any pair that cannot connect.

What v11 fixes: two owner calls from playtest. (1) The congruent rule was not stated on the info panel: Classic said “4 equal pieces” and “every equal division wins”, so a division into two 25% bars and two 25% squares looked like a win but failed Check — the checker has always required congruent pieces (same size and same shape), and the panel never said so. The intro, the Classic objective, every “identical pieces” objective, the canvas hint and the win/fail messages now state “same size and same shape” explicitly, and Classic adds “Same size alone is not enough”. Equal area remains the stated exception (same size only). (2) Diagonal is dropped from Batch A: its board’s triangles overlap, so it cannot be cut into 4 pieces of 4 triangle-units — the preset is removed from the picker and is no longer part of Batch A (Classic + 9 remain).

What v12 fixes: the owner tapped the square’s top-left corner, then its bottom-right corner, got “No grid line connects those two points” — yet the dashed preview ran straight through the intermediate grid dots (1,1), (2,2), (3,3), so the points looked connected. Two faults: (1) a convex corner touches no cuttable grid line at all — the only lines there are the shape’s outline — so the v10 router, which walked only cuttable edges, could never start or end a cut at any corner; (2) when no route existed the preview still drew a straight dashed line directly between the two taps, visually inventing the diagonal connection the message denied. The router now also walks the outline, at 2.5× cost so a real grid route always wins where one exists, and commits only the grid-line part of the route: opposite corners now produce a stepped cut along the grid lines, and the preview shows it exactly before the tap lands. The preview draws only the actual routed cut, segment by segment — no route, no line. The dots on a diagonal are not connected to each other diagonally; they are joined only by horizontal and vertical lines, and the hint under the board now says so.

The scoring (1,000 for a win, −1 per second floored at 200, −100 per hint, −50 per mistake, −50 per restart, countdown scored on seconds left), the 3-mistake lose rule and the hint behaviour are proposals from the variant matrix, not locks — the owner locks them at the brief.

Owner-locked percentage split, as built:

  • Progress % — always on in the stats panel (share of the shape separated into detached pieces). Not a variant.
  • Piece-share labels — every detached piece shows its actual share of the shape (“25%” on each piece of an equal 4-way split), so pieces of different shapes but equal area read as equal.
  • Equality score — rule variant: the Equality score preset ends any complete division with an equality % and scales the points by it, instead of pass/fail. Check also works mid-division as a self-check: it reports the current equality % without ending the run or counting a mistake.

Boards (all except Classic reuse the Python-verified shapes from boards.json):

  • Classic is the owner’s example — an empty square cut into 4 identical pieces (same size and same shape). A square has many valid identical divisions and every one wins; the hint suggests one (four small squares).
  • Pieces 2, Pieces 5, Piece size 4, Symbols, One tree, Shape given, Countdown and No hint: the stored division is unique (exact-cover count 1) — but in v4 the checker still accepts any division that satisfies the stated rule, stored answer or not.
  • Equal area: 6 valid partitions were counted — any equal-area partition wins. This is the one preset where pieces only need the same size, not the same shape.

The seed L-tromino puzzle itself has 2 valid divisions, so it is not used as a stored-answer board.

Equal-area Easy grading is still an open problem: this demo accepts any valid equal-area partition and does not pretend to grade Easy vs trivial.

The preset picker is dev-only. The player surface will never show variant names. There are no levels, no progression and no unlocking.

## The new foundation

**Time Strata** is a proposed Memory baseline built around layered access. Four stacks
hold hidden cards; only each nonempty stack's top card is playable. Matching two tops
removes them and exposes older layers. The stack structure changes legal access,
rather than merely arranging an ordinary deck differently.

Compared with `memory:classic`, players cannot select an arbitrary hidden card.
Compared with the accepted Split Dual-Garden baseline, there are four changing top
surfaces rather than two fully accessible zones. Neither same-stack second flips nor
covered-card access is legal. Accepted rules and replay identities are preserved.

## Proposed rules and complete review states

- Four stacks, labelled I–IV, retain their order on every viewport. Supported fixture
  deals have 6, 8 or 10 pairs, giving each stack 3, 4 or 5 cards.
- All cards start face down. Covered layers show neutral backs and a count, without
  exposing their symbols. A top flip reveals one shape and its text name.
- A second flip comes from a different, nonempty stack. Equal symbols match; different
  symbols mismatch. Two reveals complete one attempt. A mismatch also increments the
  mismatch counter.
- A single reveal can be cancelled by its card or **Cancel selection**, spending no
  attempt. Covered layers have no button or keyboard target.
- After two reveals, the tops remain visible until **Continue**. Input is held during
  this readable outcome state. Continue lifts a match, or closes a mismatch without
  changing either stack. Newly exposed tops remain face down.
- Empty stacks retain their wells and labels. Clearing all four stacks shows **All
  layers cleared**, attempts and mismatches, and focuses Reset.
- **Reset deal** restores the same deal and clears pending selection, outcome,
  counters, guide and cosmetic motion. **New deal** advances a repeatable fixture deal.
  Changing pair count starts a fresh round at that depth.
- **Show example** uses the normal handlers for an exposed pair, its removal/new layer,
  and a mismatch with unchanged depth. Each example step is controlled by the reader;
  ordinary play can resume at any point.

There is no proposed timer, survival budget or score formula in this baseline review.
The deliberate Continue flow and face-down opening are preview design choices awaiting
owner review; no production opening, timing or scoring change is authorized here.

## Solvable construction

The fixture inserts distinct symbols using this repeated pair-of-stacks schedule:
`(I, II)`, `(III, IV)`, `(I, III)`, `(II, IV)`. For each symbol it pushes one copy onto
each scheduled stack. A small integer-only fixture shuffle determines symbol order.

Reverse insertion order always supplies a legal sequence of equal top removals: the
last remaining inserted symbol has both copies exposed. Removing another already
exposed pair also preserves this property. Four stack heights are balanced at 3/4/5
for 6/8/10 pairs. No search, retry or rejection loop is needed.

For an unshuffled six-pair example, top → bottom is I: E/C/A, II: E/D/A,
III: F/C/B, IV: F/D/B. One legal solution is F, E, D, C, B, A.

This demonstrates fixture solvability only. Production generation/difficulty,
immutable identity, headless replay, scoring and fairness must be designed and checked
in a separately authorized local integration; the fixture is not engine code or
six-runtime evidence.

## Presentation and accessibility

The fixture reuses the site's light tokens and local Nunito font, flat card fills,
shape symbols and text labels. Portrait 390×844 uses a 2×2 stack layout; landscape
844×390 and desktop 1280×800 use four stacks in a row. Native top-card buttons and
play controls have at least 44px touch targets. Focus moves to Continue after an
attempt and to an available top after resolution. Status changes are announced.

Reduced motion or a missing animation asset leaves gameplay available. Animation is
isolated behind a fixture motion wrapper using the existing anime.js 4.5.0 v4 API;
no motion callback drives game state. Optional rule help can expand for review.

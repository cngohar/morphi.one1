## Shared board and turn contract

Four labelled stacks retain their identity across portrait, landscape and desktop.
Supported review deals contain 6/8/10 pairs, initially 3/4/5 cards per stack.
Opening cards stay face down. Ordinary flips can select only exposed tops;
covered layers have no interactive targets. Sample Shelf additionally offers the
previously lifted top as one visible selectable sample. Clue-only cards are labelled
and never selectable.

A single selection can be cancelled without an attempt. Two reveals create a
held outcome; Continue resolves it. Every confirmed removal exposes older cards.
Utilities use a shared Source stack selector and a mode-specific Lift to shelf,
Return sample, Cycle top or Move top button. Empty Well adds an Empty destination
selector. Utilities work at idle, spend no attempt or miss and cannot run during
an outcome, clue phase or single selection. Reset restores the same deal and clears all state;
New deal, mode changes and depth changes start fresh. Empty wells retain their
labels. Final removal requires Continue; completion shows attempts/misses.
No wall-clock callback changes the board or resolves an outcome.

## Ten concrete rule differences

1. **Linked Tops — `memory:strata-linked`.** The second stack must neighbor the
   first on the logical ring I–II–III–IV–I. This order is independent of responsive
   physical placement. A non-neighbor cannot be selected and spends no attempt.
   Pair insertion `(I,II),(III,IV),(II,III),(IV,I)` balances the board; reverse
   insertion gives a full-clear route. Contrast: selecting I blocks III, while II
   and IV remain available.

2. **Level Sweep — `memory:strata-sweep`.** Only tops at the greatest remaining
   stack height are selectable. Construct complete I/II and III/IV matched levels,
   repeating the pair layout across depths. After one top pair clears,
   a lower matching pair is exposed but blocked until the other taller pair clears.
   A greatest-height pair always exists, so the gate cannot trap a valid deal.

3. **Sample Shelf — `memory:strata-shelf`.** Park one exposed top on a face-up
   shelf, exposing its next layer. Select that sample alongside the ordinary tops,
   or return it to its original stack at idle. The shelf holds one card and cannot
   be overwritten. Free cancellation preserves it. Matching a shelf/top pair removes
   both; a mismatch keeps the sample visible. Returning restores the original
   remaining order, providing a recovery route for every shelf state.

4. **Layer Cycle — `memory:strata-cycle`.** Cycle one selected stack's top to its
   bottom at idle. After balanced pair membership assignment, independently shuffle
   each stack. Copies remain in separate stacks. A player can cycle those two
   stacks until any chosen pair is exposed, then match it. This gives a solution
   from every reachable state, including after an unhelpful cycle.

5. **Empty Well — `memory:strata-empty-well`.** Move a top into a different empty
   well, exposing the next layer. Occupied destinations are illegal. Repeated
   I/II and III/IV level pairs let I/II empty early while III/IV remain deep.
   Moving III's top into I makes its covered next card playable. Transfers never
   cover existing cards and preserve the remaining dependency order.

6. **Deep Glimpse — `memory:strata-glimpse`.** Selecting one top also shows the
   immediately underlying card as a read-only clue. Hide it on cancellation or
   the second flip. A one-card stack has no underlying clue. This changes the
   information available about a covered layer without permitting covered flips.

7. **Chain Reaction — `memory:strata-chain`.** After a confirmed match, equal
   newly exposed tops in those same wells become another held match. Each chained
   pair spends one attempt and needs its own Continue; unequal tops stay face down.
   Repeated I/II and III/IV levels make multi-layer chains reachable. The final
   chained pair still needs confirmation; motion never removes it automatically.

8. **Layer Echo — `memory:strata-echo`.** After a match is removed, show the newly
   exposed tops in its affected wells as read-only clues. A separate Continue
   closes them without an attempt. All tops and other controls that alter play
   are held during this clue phase. Skip the phase when neither affected well has
   a remaining card. This differs from Deep Glimpse's covered clue during a single
   selection and from Chain Reaction's counted matching outcome.

9. **Slipping Tops — `memory:strata-slip`.** Mismatch Continue sinks both revealed
   tops to the bottoms of their own stacks. An idle one-stack Cycle is available
   for recovery. Independently shuffled stacks retain each pair in distinct wells;
   cycling can always expose both copies again. A one-card stack's cycle leaves
   it unchanged. No timed fall or hidden reorder occurs.

10. **Excavation Order — `memory:strata-order`.** Display a required symbol sequence
    in reverse insertion order. Equal tops clear only when they match the current
    target. An equal off-target pair creates a held order-blocked outcome: one
    attempt and one miss, with no removal or cursor advance after Continue. The
    required pair is always exposed after its predecessors clear. Contrast: E is
    exposed but blocked while F is the current target in the six-pair example.

All ten change legal access, available information, stack order or turn outcomes
relative to Time Strata. Colors, symbols, new seeds and ordinary deals are not
counted as variants. Candidate names/IDs are reserved in this brief only.

## Solvability and review routes

For the baseline six-pair example, tops → bottoms are I: E/C/A, II: E/D/A,
III: F/C/B, IV: F/D/B. Reverse insertion clears F, E, D, C, B, A.
Linked Tops uses its ring schedule; Sweep uses complete matched levels. Both
retain constructive reverse-insertion routes. Order additionally checks that
exact target sequence.

Sample Shelf example: park E from I, clear F then C, return E, and clear E, D,
B, A. Returning a sample always restores a constructive remaining board.
Empty Well example: repeated I/II and III/IV levels let E, C, A empty I/II;
move F from III to I, then clear F from I/IV and D, B from III/IV.
Chain Reaction's repeated levels clear E → C → A and F → D → B, with one
Continue per pair. Glimpse and Echo preserve the baseline removal order.

The preview uses fixed pairing schedules with shuffled symbol order; Sweep,
Empty Well and Chain repeat I/II and III/IV levels. These schedules demonstrate
rules and solvability. They are not a production difficulty/diversity or fairness
claim; those choices remain part of separately selected app integration.

Cycle and Slip rely on separate-stack membership, rather than an equality-only
greedy solver. Rotate the two stacks containing a target symbol until both
copies are exposed. Removing that pair preserves separate membership for all
remaining symbols. The focused runner must prove this route independently and
exercise both manual cycling and a slipping mismatch through ordinary UI actions.

## 2. Rules — from global research

Source: global One-line research (12 Nov 2026, `workspace/research_notes/one-line-drawing-global-research-20261112/report.md`). The rules below are this brief's own statements; no external file is required to build from them.

- **Core loop:** a figure made of dots (vertices) joined by lines (edges) is presented whole on a centered phone board; the player starts on a dot and drags one continuous stroke through the figure until every line is covered exactly once. Solo Eulerian-trail drawing.
- **Legal stroke:** an Euler trail uses every edge of a connected graph exactly once; vertices may be revisited/passed through more than once, but no edge may be repeated. Crossings are NOT vertices unless marked with a dot: lines that cross without a dot are separate, and you can only turn where lines meet at a dot (research §1; demo-proved by the Pentagram).
- **Start/end rule:** 0 odd-degree vertices = closed circuit, start anywhere and finish where you started; exactly 2 odd = open trail, must start at one odd vertex and finish at the other; more than 2 odd = impossible (Euler 1736; Königsberg: 7 bridges, degrees 3, 5, 3, 3).
- **Lift (LOCKED at acceptance — A1):** lift-reset — lifting the finger/pen before the figure is complete resets the whole drawing, so the player tries a different start/route. Lift-resume is Batch A #9. In the `two-stroke` preset the single permitted lift ends the current stroke instead of resetting (the preset allows exactly 2 strokes); a lift beyond the stroke budget is refused, not a loss. After the lift, the player starts stroke 2 at an eligible dot — one of the two odd vertices remaining in the remainder — chosen by the player; the first trace of stroke 2 carries its start dot in `value` (§4, Rev 2).
- **Retracing (L7 LOCKED):** retracing a drawn edge is blocked by the input — the move is rejected, the stroke stays where it is, no penalty. It is not an instant fail and not a reset. The rejection message must be shown (demo caveat: never cleanly verified live — §9).
- **Dead end (L6 LOCKED):** arriving at a dot with no unused edges left while edges remain undrawn is a LOSE for the round — score 0, no daily-board row (§5). This is Morphi's rule, not classic play (classic has no lose state; the remedy was undo/restart). Undo BEFORE the dead end is the rescue: backtrack, then try another edge. A wrong early choice that strands the figure (taking a bridge too early, Fleury's rule) is the trap; undo is the recovery.
- **Win:** every edge covered its required number of times (once, or twice for ×2 edges in `double-trace`) in the allowed strokes. **Lose:** dead end (L6), countdown timeout (§3 #6), or wrong verdict (§3 #10).
- **Undo (L8 LOCKED):** free in the baseline — drag back along the drawn line rewinds one step at a time, and an Undo button does the same. Undo is a replayed action (§4). Undo cannot rescue a round after a dead-end lose has triggered (the round is already lost).
- **Restart:** a replayed action (§4) that restores the empty stroke, costs −50 (§5), and does NOT reset elapsed ranked time or hints used. Restart does not re-roll the figure (same seed → same figure).
- **Hint:** tiered — first the valid start points, then the next correct line/step, then the full path (research §5). Deterministic: the hint follows the Hierholzer witness from the current position (§4). If the current partial trail cannot be completed (the route is stranded), the hint diagnoses it — "stranded, undo to backtrack" — and reveals no step (demo behaviour). Baseline hint budget is max 3 per round (specified in this brief, not owner-locked — §10).
- **Verdict:** in the `verdict` preset only, the player may declare the figure impossible instead of drawing it (§3 #10). A correct verdict wins 500; a wrong verdict loses (0, no row).
- **Controls:** one-finger press-and-drag along the lines/dots (tap dot-to-dot also works); drag back to undo; buttons Undo, Hint, Restart (Undo/Hint flanking a prominent Restart, demo restyle); keyboard: arrows move a dot cursor, Enter/Space traces to the selected dot, U undo, H hint, R restart, N next puzzle after a win (ResultScreen N shortcut already exists — verify it is present, do not re-add it). Dots are 44–48 pt tap targets (research §5); feedback must appear above or beside the contact point, never under the finger.
- **Style reference:** Morphi branding is pale `#eef2f7`, navy `#1b2433`, Nunito, orange-red `#e5532d`, blue `#2f6fed`, teal `#009a86`, violet `#7d61ae`, vector only. Demo restyle: soft white board card, pill Lines/Time/Hints stats, odd dots with solid orange-red ring + dashed halo, compact numbered How to Play strip under the board. ## 4. Generation, determinism, fairness

- **RNG:** `createRng(seed)` (`src/lib/engine/rng.ts`, SplitMix64 v1 — verified at HEAD `806c33f`: `createRng(seed: string)` with `nextU64()`; `shuffle(rng, items)` exported from the same file, used by Connect). All draws from one stream in fixed order. Integer-only. **Banned in logic/manifest:** `Math.random`, `Date.now`, `performance.now`, `new Date`, float math (`Math.sqrt/pow/sin/...`). Enforced by ESLint + `scripts/check-boundaries.ts`; a float solver diverges across the 6 runtimes.
- **Size bands (Nonogram pattern — bands are difficulty bands, never variants):** the band is the FIRST draw from the seed stream inside `createRound` (Nonogram precedent, `nonogram/logic.ts:481-483`: `createRng(seed)`, then a band draw before any other draw), so the same seed + variant always reconstructs the same board at issuance, resume and save — no mode parameter exists on `createRound` or `dailySeedOk` (`contract.ts:116-125`), and none is added (Rev 1). The band draw yields an edge count in 6–34 (Lakolam Kids 6–10 through Hard 25–34). `dailySeedOk` accepts a candidate daily seed ONLY if its drawn band is 17–24 edges (Lakolam Medium; specified in this brief, not owner-locked — §10); unlimited accepts whatever band the seed draws. Edge count alone is never a variant (§5 of the matrix).
- **Pipeline (generate-then-verify — research §4; Lakolam parity-repair + Nothing Serious reject-and-regenerate):**
  1. Draw a connected graph from the seed stream (lattice walk, Lakolam pattern), in the band for the round.
  2. Check connectivity + parity class for the preset (0/2 odd for 1-stroke; 4 odd for two-stroke; 0 odd for closed-loop; >2 odd, 4–6, for a deliberately impossible verdict figure; directed indegree/outdegree balance + weak connectivity for one-way).
  3. Compute a Hierholzer witness trail in O(V + E) and re-walk it to verify it uses every required traversal exactly once; for one-way, run directed Hierholzer on the directed (and doubled, where Axis B applies) multigraph; for crossings-are-junctions, run it on the augmented graph. For `two-stroke` the witness is a TWO-TRAIL decomposition (stroke 1 + stroke 2, Rev 1 — see gate (e2); Rev 2: stroke 1 runs odd→odd, stroke 2 starts at an eligible dot — one of the two remaining odds — not where stroke 1 ended). For a deliberately impossible verdict figure there is NO trail witness; the proof is the parity certificate (gate (e3)).
  4. Reject and regenerate (with an offset seed) any figure that fails a gate below.
- **Fairness gates (the hard rule — reject and regenerate on any failure):** a figure is issued only if ALL hold: (a) connected, all nonzero-degree vertices in one component; (b) parity class matches the preset (§4 step 2); (c) the Hierholzer witness re-walks every required traversal exactly once; (d) **not already solved** — the starting (empty) stroke is not a win; (e) the figure has at least one trap (Rev 3 — redefined; the Rev 2 wording tested a SINGLE trail, which is wrong for `two-stroke` — a position can lack a single trail and still be solvable with the remaining lift: square with both diagonals, partial trail A→B, has no single trail from B yet lifts to stroke 2 C→B→D→C→A→D): there exists a reachable partial trail — possibly empty, of any length — ending at a dot c, and a legal next edge from c, such that after traversing that edge the remaining graph admits NO valid completion within the preset's remaining stroke budget. A completion is an ordered list of trails covering all remaining required traversals: the first trail starts at the new current dot (it may be empty — lift immediately — when a lift remains), each later trail starts at an eligible start dot for that preset (`two-stroke`: a remaining odd vertex of the remainder at that point, or any vertex if 0 odds; one-stroke presets: no later trail exists, so this reduces exactly to the Rev 2 single-trail test), and each trail respects the preset's direction rules (one-way edges only in their allowed direction — the trail test is directed for one-way figures, using the same directed-Hierholzer machinery as §4 step 3, not merely undirected parity). The partial trail carries its stroke index; the remaining budget is (strokes allowed − strokes used, counting the current stroke). For a 0-odd figure the first move can never be the trap: every edge of a connected Eulerian graph lies on a cycle, so removing it leaves the graph connected with exactly its two endpoints odd, and a trail from the arrival dot always exists — so the witness partial trail has length ≥ 1 there (bowtie example, CORRECTED in Rev 3 — the Rev 2 example was wrong: start A1, trace A1→C, then C→A2 does NOT strand the player, because A2→B2→C→B1→A1 covers all remaining edges; the genuine trap is completing one triangle first — A1→C→B1→A1 strands the player at A1 with the second triangle unreachable — verified by exhaustive check, see rev3-findings.md). For a 2-odd figure the trap may already be the first move (a starting bridge — e.g. the single edge joining two triangles in a dumbbell); for a 4-odd figure it may also be the first move (8-edge witness figure with edges AD, BD, CE, AB, AC, BF, DE, BC: start B, first edge B→D strands at D even with the lift — verified by exhaustive check, see rev3-findings.md). A bridge in the STARTING graph is NOT the test on its own: a connected all-even-degree graph has no bridges at all, so a starting-graph test would make `closed-loop` unpassable. Gate (e) does NOT apply to verdict-impossible figures: an impossible board starts unsolvable by design, so "no trail" is vacuously true at every position and the trap test is meaningless there — those figures are gated by the parity certificate (e3) only. Verdict-drawable figures are gated like drawing boards. (e2) `two-stroke` only (Rev 2 — corrected; the Rev 1 wording required stroke 2 to start where stroke 1 ended, which NO figure can satisfy): the witness is a two-trail decomposition into two non-empty trails. Stroke 1 runs from one odd vertex to another odd vertex (consuming two of the four odds, so the remainder has exactly the other two odds); stroke 2 runs on the remainder from one of those two remaining odd vertices to the other. Stroke 2 starts at an ELIGIBLE dot — one of the two remaining odds — NOT necessarily where stroke 1 ended (it cannot start there: stroke 1's end is even in the remainder, and a trail covering the remainder must start at one of its odd vertices). The legal-stop check is kept: the lift that ends stroke 1 is legal only if the remainder admits a valid completion within the remaining budget after the lift (for `two-stroke`, a single trail from an eligible start dot — the same budget-aware completion test as gate (e), not a separate rule); ending stroke 1 where the remainder is untraversable is a loss at that moment (the lift is a committed decision). "Stroke 1 runs odd→odd" above describes the witness decomposition, not a play constraint — the operative play rule is this legal-stop check. Satisfiability witness: the square with both diagonals (4 odds, 6 edges) — stroke 1 A→B, stroke 2 C→B→D→C→A→D, starting at C while stroke 1 ended at B (verified by exhaustive check, see rev2-findings.md) — and the square passes the corrected gate (e) via a stroke-2 trap (stroke 1 A→B→C, lift at C, stroke 2 D→B strands at B, which has no unused edges left — verified by exhaustive check, see rev3-findings.md). (e3) verdict-impossible figures: no trail witness is computed; the certificate is the parity count — the figure is connected and has >2 odd-degree vertices (4–6), with the odd-degree vertex list stored as the certificate. A verdict-impossible candidate that is disconnected, or has ≤2 odd vertices, is rejected. Drawable verdict figures need a normal trail witness. (f) for verdict figures, the impossible rate is ~1 in 5 figures (Lakolam rate, specified in this brief, not owner-locked) — verdict rounds present a drawable or an impossible figure, never only one kind. Record the rejection tally per gate in tests; it is the tuning signal.

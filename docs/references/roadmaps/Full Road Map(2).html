Morphi.One · Roadmap

Every puzzle family, A to Z — most-played to nobody-plays-it… yet

A filterable roadmap of 182 puzzle families — that means 182 puzzle types, not 182 games (see “Family vs Game” directly below). Built from global research (2023–2025 published data, report dated 25 Oct 2026). Because Morphi deals a puzzle the player never chose, even an obscure family earns its place — it arrives as a surprise for one round. Priorities below follow the evidence, not fashion: mass-market first, loyal niches as the retention library, and the forgotten ones revived only with their original problem fixed.

Morphi today — only 3 of the 182 families are in progress as Morphi games, the other 179 are roadmap entries only, not built and not playable: Block (block-fit family) and Memory (memory pairs family) are live in v1 · Connect (Flow family) is a built prototype awaiting acceptance. No build or integration notes here — this page is only about what to build, in what order, and why players stay or leave.

How to read the labels

Priorities: High Priority Medium Low Priority Later Nobody Plays It… Yet

Band is not Priority. Band = how widely a puzzle is played in the real world today (see the full explanation directly below). Priority = Morphi's recommended build order. They are different: a Band D puzzle can still be worth building later, and a Band A puzzle is not automatically next.

Family vs Game — what is actually counted here?

Short answer: nobody built 182 games. This roadmap is a catalogue of 182 families — the original solo-puzzle research plus the multiplayer-puzzle families added from the companion multiplayer research (report dated 26 Oct 2026). Only 3 of them exist in Morphi so far (2 live, 1 prototype) — the other 179 are names on a list, to be built one at a time later.

Family = a type

1 family = 1 set of rules

A family is a puzzle type — one set of rules for how a round works.

Examples of families: Sudoku, Match-3, Water Sort, Block-fit, Flow.

The A–Z index at the bottom of this page lists 182 families — not 182 separate commercial games, and not 182 built / playable games.

Game = a specific title

Many games can share one family

A game (in the commercial sense) is one specific, named title made by someone, sitting inside a family.

Example: Royal Match and Candy Crush are two different commercial games — both are example games in the Match-3 family. That is still only one family in this roadmap.

Wherever a commercial title appears on this page — Block Blast!, Royal Match, Wordle, Flow Free and so on — read it as “example game(s) in this family”. It is there only as evidence for how popular its family is, not as a separate roadmap entry and not as a Morphi game.

In Morphi

1 family = 1 future Morphi game

In Morphi terms, one family becomes one Morphi game — and inside that one game there are unlimited seeded instances / rounds, each dealt fresh from a seed. A family is never just one single puzzle.

Morphi today — only 3 families in progress:
• Block — block-fit family — Live
• Memory — memory pairs family — Live
• Connect — Flow family — Prototype

The other 179 families are roadmap entries only — not built.

Worked example, so it can't be misread: Family = Block-fit (the type: drag blocks, clear lines). Example commercial game in this family = Block Blast! (someone else's title, used here as popularity evidence). Morphi game in this family = Block (Morphi's own version, live). Three different things — type, their game, our game — for one single family.

Solo, Multiplayer or Both — now from research, not guesses

An honest correction first: an earlier version of this page tagged families by potential — what multiplayer form a family could take (same-seed races, turn-based versus and so on). Those tags were educated guesses, and they have been replaced. Every family now carries exactly one research-based label — Solo, Multiplayer or Both — meaning how that family is actually played in the real world, from the companion multiplayer research (84 multiplayer families in seven forms, report dated 26 Oct 2026), using the same [P] / [E] / [R] / [ABSENT] evidence discipline as the solo research.

Solo

Played solo in the real world

No played multiplayer form was found — no official mode, no championship, no versus or co-op app with real players [ABSENT]. A multiplayer version may be theoretically possible, but nobody plays it that way, so it does not count. Examples: Flow / Connect, Water Sort, Block-fit and the sort puzzles are all Solo only by this evidence.

Multiplayer

Played with or against other people

The family's real, played form is multiplayer — versus, co-op, party, async turns or team play. Some of these families were born multiplayer and were missing from a solo-only roadmap entirely; they are now in the A–Z list. Examples: Kahoot!, Words With Friends, Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes, 4-player Mahjong.

Both

Played solo and multiplayer

Both forms genuinely exist and are played. The multiplayer form is often a race on the same puzzle (a championship, a race app, a shared daily score) rather than head-to-head interaction. Examples: Sudoku, Nonogram, Crosswords, Jigsaw, Minesweeper, Japanese logic — and Memory pairs, whose multiplayer form is its original form.

Band D — No data found

Dead multiplayer is labelled, not hidden

Several multiplayer families in the list are dead — Draw Something (collapsed as a mass game), QuizUp (servers off 24 Mar 2021), HQ Trivia (ceased 14 Feb 2020), Tetris Battle / Friends (closed 31 May 2019), Minesweeper Flags (delisted 2024). Their cards keep their peak figures and their death dates, because why they died is roadmap evidence.

Counts are generated from the same dataset as the A–Z cards below, so they always match the list.

This label is an attribute, not a priority. It does not change any family's Band or Priority. Morphi today is still solo, and multiplayer remains a parked / later feature in the owner's plans (with a future slot already reserved in the profile design) — this section simply stops the roadmap pretending multiplayer puzzles don't exist. Two near-identical names must never be merged: Mahjong Solitaire is Solo only, while 4-player Mahjong is Multiplayer — different families. And one honest limit on the label Both for Merge: it is weak — only the 2048 / Suika branch has played versus forms; progression-merge games (Merge Mansion type) are Solo only.

What is a “Band”? — Band A, B, C and D explained

Band = popularity band. Every family in the A–Z list is placed in one band based on the research evidence for how many people actually play it today — downloads, active players, plays, revenue and chart presence in 2023–2025. For Band D the “evidence” is the opposite: no such data was found at all (see its card below). It is not a difficulty rating, not a quality rating, and not Morphi's build order.

Band A — Mass

Most-played

Huge global audience. Hundreds of millions of downloads, billions of plays, or top-of-chart revenue.

Examples: Block-fit, Match-3, Wordle, Connections, Tetris.

18 families start in Band A (including A/B borderline cases).

Band B — Medium

Medium-played

Proven mainstream audience, but smaller than Band A. Typically tens of millions of installs, or a strong, visible app / web presence.

Examples: Water Sort, Mahjong Solitaire, Nonogram, Word Search, Jigsaw, Triple Tile's neighbours like Ball Sort.

64 families start in Band B (including B/C and B/D borderline cases).

Band C — Niche-but-loyal

Less-played · loyal

Small audience, but devoted and long-lived. Often no mass chart numbers — loyalty shows up in syndication, championships, books and decades of repeat play instead.

Examples: Slitherlink, Kakuro, KenKen, Minesweeper, Sokoban, Tangram.

102 families start in Band C (including C/D borderline cases).

Band D — No data found

No data found

The research found no modern downloads, users or retention data for these families anywhere — labelled [ABSENT]. That is not proof nobody plays them: no data is not the same as no players, and a small audience may simply be unmeasured. Most of these families also faded for a documented reason: too hard to teach, too slow, needed paper or special pieces, language-locked, or boring once solved.

Examples: Yajisan-Kazusan, Diagramless Crossword, Lights Out, Burr puzzle, Sator Square.

58 families are Band D.

What about labels like A/B, B/C or C/D? The puzzle sits between two bands. For example, A/B means mass in one measure or market but medium in another; B/C means medium overall with a niche, loyal core; C/D means niche overall, shading into no-data-found, with only a small loyal remnant. One special case is labelled B/D — Arrowword: Band B in much of Europe, Band D in the US. On each card below, the badge now spells the band out in full — for example “Band A — Mass” — so you don't need to memorise the letters.

Multiplayer retention — the headline is an absence: no publisher has published a multiplayer-vs-solo retention, session-length or sessions-per-day split for any puzzle game [ABSENT]. Timeline

Two views: how these puzzles lived and died in the real world, and where Morphi's roadmap goes next. In the Morphi view, tap a phase to filter the A–Z list to the games in that phase.

Before AD 62

Sator Square — Pompeii

The famous word square survives from Pompeii as an artefact / charm, not as a living game [P]. Band D today.

1880

The 15-Puzzle fad

Sliding tiles sweep the world — then fade. Half of all random 15-Puzzle setups are mathematically unsolvable [P], one reason the faithful form sits in Band D today.

1924

Diagramless crossword — by accident

The grid-less crossword begins as a 1924 accident [P]: the solver must rebuild the black squares too. Too slow and hard to teach — Band D today.

1978

Simon

The light-and-tone sequence toy becomes a hardware icon. No modern chart or MAU data exists for Simon-type play [ABSENT].

1992–2009

Minesweeper, bundled with Windows

Free on almost every PC [P] — then unbundled in Windows 8, and its mass audience goes with the distribution, not the game.

1995

Lights Out

A dedicated electronic toy. It collapses once the “chase the lights” trick is known [P] — a lesson in solved-once puzzles.

2012 → today

Flow Free — the long tail

Flow-type play proves durable: 230M Play installs and 2,500+ free levels [E/P]. Morphi's Connect prototype is this family's form.

2023

NYT kills Digits after ~4 months

Even the New York Times retires a numbers daily quickly when it doesn't hold — distribution alone doesn't save a puzzle.

29 Aug 2024

NYT Vertex discontinued [P]

Another NYT experiment ends — the daily-games catalogue is ruthlessly pruned, which is why Morphi's suspense model (no catalogue to prune) is different.

18 Aug 2025

NYT launches Pips

Domino logic redesigned to be taught visually — the modern template for reviving a Band D idea: fix the teach cost, keep the logic.

c. 1800 · 1817–18

Tangram — the first global multiplayer puzzle fad

Invented / popularised in China c. 1800 [E]; the 1817–18 craze sweeps Europe and America as parlour play with informal races [E]. Tangram races are still a played form today.

1978

American Crossword Puzzle Tournament founded

Will Shortz founds the ACPT in Stamford with 149 contestants [E] — crossword solving becomes an organised race. It grows to 926 contestants in the 2025 report and 915 in-person + 319 virtual in the 2026 official history [E/P], including an official Pairs division.

1990

Nintendo World Championships — Tetris on a national tour

The tour visits 29 US cities; finals combine Mario, Rad Racer and a Tetris score leg [E]. Competitive Tetris is mass entertainment a decade before online versus.

1991

Puyo Puyo launches

Versus chain-puzzling becomes a Japanese institution [E] — still running: JESU-certified pro licences, a Sega Grand Prix circuit, and selection as an Asian Games 2026 esports title [P, Sega, 2 Apr 2026]. No global sales / MAU figure has ever been published [ABSENT].

1993

Tetris Battle Gaiden — character versus is born

Japan-only Super Famicom release [E]: clear lines, spend orbs on character attacks. With Puyo Puyo (1991), Panel de Pon (1995) and Super Puzzle Fighter II Turbo (1996), the 1990s codify garbage-battle design.

Jul 2009

Words With Friends launches

Async turn-based becomes mobile's default social puzzle [P/E] — turns over days, many games in parallel, notifications bringing players back.

Feb–Mar 2012

Draw Something — the spike and the collapse

50M downloads in 50 days [P, Zynga]; DAU then falls from ~14.6M peak to 10.4M within about a month [E, AppData] and the studio is closed in June 2013. Social virality demonstrably did not retain it.

20 May 2014

Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection shuts down

Online play for Tetris DS, Dr. Mario and every DS / Wii puzzle title ends at a stroke [P, Nintendo] — a platform-shutdown death, not a popularity death.

13 Feb 2019

Tetris 99 — 99-player battle-royale Tetris

Launches free for Nintendo Switch Online members [P, Nintendo]. Its total player count has never been published [ABSENT].

31 May 2019

Tetris Online, Inc. ceases operations

Tetris Friends and Tetris Battle (once 16M MAU [E]) close the same day [P/E]; the community migrates to unofficial TETR.IO and Jstris.

14 Feb 2020

HQ Trivia ceases operations

The appointment live-trivia show — record 2.38M concurrent players on 28 Mar 2018 [E] — ends when investors stop funding it [E]. The format decayed as prizes split among too many winners shrank to cents.

24 Mar 2021

QuizUp servers taken offline

Death by monetisation failure despite scale [P/E]: 20M users and over 1 billion matches by 2014, ~$55M+ raised, sold on for $7.5M in 2016, then discontinued.

2026

Puyo Puyo at the Asian Games

A 1991 versus puzzler is selected as an Asian Games 2026 esports title [P, Sega] — the longest-lived multiplayer puzzle form on this page reaches multi-sport-event status.

Rules the research implies for every Morphi round: no colour-only coding (always pair colour with pattern/letter/shape), undo is a retention feature not a booster, never sell the extra tube/shuffle the level design made necessary, no interruption inside a >5-minute solve, and label hard levels “Hard” honestly — difficulty spikes are the #1 complaint in the #1 genre.

Who plays, and where

Puzzle players are majority women over 35 — US 75% women for matching + word/brain/board, UK 74%, Japan 66%, Korea 73% [P, Udonis multi-market report]. A rewarded-panel study found 81% female, 84% play for relaxation, 54% for escapism [P-panel, Mistplay 2024 — panel skew, not a universe figure]. Players hold 2–3 games, prefer short sessions and solo play, and mostly do not identify as “gamers”. No vendor publishes a clean gender split by subgenre for 2023–25 [ABSENT] — directional only: match-3 / merge / jigsaw / word skew strongest female 35–64; block-fit and tricky puzzles are broader/younger; Sudoku / logic / speedcubing are more balanced or male-skewing in competition scenes.

Downloads map ≠ revenue map. India is the #1 mobile-games download market (~17% of global game downloads, Oct 2024 [E, Sensor Tower]), then US ~8%, Brazil ~7.5%. Block Blast downloads: Indonesia #1, then US, Vietnam, Brazil, India [E, AppMagic]. Revenue is a US / Japan / Germany / UK story (Royal Match US = 54.5% of gross [E, AppMagic]). A web, SEO-led model follows the download map; a subscription model follows the revenue map.

Earlier cut, different measure — ESA 2025: 59% of all US players play puzzle, and Boomer / Silent-generation players prefer puzzle at 73% [P, ESA 2025]. The ESA 2026 figures at the top of this page are mobile- and PC-specific cuts, so the two editions are not directly comparable.

The roadmap in tiers

Every tier below is computed from the same priority labels as the A–Z list — the family names and the count in each heading come straight from the list itself, so this section always matches the cards.

Why Band D died — the pattern

Too hard to teach in <30 seconds (Tentai Show, Shakashaka) · too slow for a phone session (diagramless, acrostic, barred cryptic) · needs paper or special pieces (burr, puzzle boxes, Simon hardware) · language-locked (Sator square, crossnumber) · solved once the trick is known (Lights Out “chase the lights”, 15-Puzzle parity — half of all random 15-Puzzle setups are mathematically unsolvable [P]) · or lost distribution (Minesweeper unbundled in Windows 8; NYT Digits killed after ~4 months; NYT Vertex discontinued 29 Aug 2024 [P]).
